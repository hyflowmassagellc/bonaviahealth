Acupuncture: what science says — and what it doesn't

21 November 2026 · 5 min read · Bonavia Health

Acupuncture is the best-known part of Chinese medicine outside China. It is also the most researched. So what does the science actually show? Here is a balanced summary based on the US National Institutes of Health.

What acupuncture is

Acupuncture is a technique in which practitioners insert fine needles into the skin at specific points to treat health problems. In the United States, national survey data show it is used most often for pain — back, joint or neck pain.

Where the evidence is encouraging

  • Back and neck pain. Reviews summarized by the National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) found that the pain-relieving effect of acupuncture was comparable to that of common anti-inflammatory painkillers (NSAIDs). Evidence for chronic low-back pain was judged moderate in quality.
  • Tension headaches. There is moderate- to low-quality evidence that acupuncture may reduce how often they occur.
  • Nausea from chemotherapy. According to the National Cancer Institute, this is where the strongest evidence in cancer care comes from: a 2013 review of 41 randomized trials found acupuncture helped.

Where it is uncertain

  • For some conditions, real and "sham" (simulated) acupuncture give similar results — for example, in studies of hip osteoarthritis reviewed by NCCIH.
  • For cancer pain and many other symptoms, results are mixed, often because studies are small.
  • How acupuncture works is not fully understood. Researchers see possible effects on the nervous system and other tissues, as well as non-specific (placebo) effects.

What acupuncture does not do

There is no reliable evidence that acupuncture cures cancer, infections or other serious diseases. It can be a useful complement for symptoms such as pain and nausea — never a replacement for diagnosis and medical treatment.

Staying safe

  • Acupuncture is generally considered safe when performed by a trained, licensed practitioner using sterile, single-use needles.
  • Tell the practitioner if you take blood thinners, have a pacemaker, are pregnant or have a bleeding disorder.
  • Mild soreness or small bruises are common; serious problems are rare but can happen if it is done incorrectly.

Acupuncture in China

In China, acupuncture is provided by licensed doctors in TCM hospitals and departments, often alongside rehabilitation and pain medicine. If you would like to include acupuncture in your treatment or recovery, we can find a suitable hospital and arrange an interpreter.
