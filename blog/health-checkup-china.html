Health check-ups in China: why more international visitors are getting one

21 November 2026 · 6 min read · Bonavia Health

A growing number of foreigners who visit China for business or tourism also book a health check-up at a major hospital. Doctors in Shanghai say that check-ups and imaging tests were, until recently, the most common reasons foreign patients came to them. Here is why — and how to do it well.

Why a check-up in China?

  • Speed. Large hospitals and check-up centres can complete blood tests, ultrasound, imaging and specialist reviews in one or two days, with results usually ready within days rather than weeks.
  • Modern equipment. Leading hospitals in Shanghai, Guangzhou, Shenzhen and Beijing are equipped with up-to-date CT, MRI and endoscopy.
  • Cost. Comprehensive packages are often much cheaper than similar check-ups in Europe or North America.
  • Easier travel. Shanghai's government notes that many foreign patients now use China's visa-free transit rules to receive medical services during a trip.

Visa options

  • 240-hour visa-free transit: since August 2026, citizens of 57 countries can stay in China for up to 10 days without a visa when travelling on to a third country.
  • Central Asia: Kazakhstan has had a mutual visa-free regime with China since November 2023, and Uzbekistan since June 2025.
  • Hainan: a separate 30-day visa-free entry policy for Hainan covers citizens of 61 countries.

Rules change and depend on your passport and route — we check the current requirements for you before you book.

What a comprehensive check-up can include

  • blood and urine tests, including liver, kidney, blood sugar, cholesterol and tumour markers;
  • ECG and heart ultrasound;
  • ultrasound of the abdomen, thyroid and, for women, breast and pelvis;
  • low-dose CT of the chest;
  • gastroscopy and colonoscopy (often under sedation);
  • optional MRI, bone density, eye and dental exams;
  • a final consultation to explain the results.

A good package is chosen according to your age, sex, family history and symptoms — more tests are not always better.

How to prepare

  • Fast for 8–12 hours before blood tests and abdominal ultrasound, unless told otherwise.
  • For colonoscopy, you will need to prepare the bowel the day before.
  • Bring previous reports and a list of medicines.
  • Plan one free day in case a follow-up test is recommended.

After the check-up

The most important part is understanding the results. Reports are often in Chinese; ask for an English summary and a consultation with a doctor. If something needs attention, decide whether to treat it in China or at home — and take all images and reports with you.

How we can help

We compare check-up packages, book the hospital, arrange transfers and an interpreter, and translate and explain your results — and, if needed, organize follow-up care.
